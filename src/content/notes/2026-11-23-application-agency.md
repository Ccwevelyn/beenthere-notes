申请季会反复碰到一个问题：要不要找中介。下面按「什么时候可以找」和「什么时候不必找」分开写。结论先说清楚：中介能帮你认路、帮你把流程摊开；它替代不了你对自己方向和课程匹配的判断，也写不出真正属于你的文书。

## 可以请中介的情况

### 1. 没有申请过，时间又紧

如果你从未递交过任何学校的申请，同时又要准备雅思、GRE，还要把毕业设计做完，手头的事叠在一起，一时分不清先后，中介可以把路径摊开：这一周该做什么、材料按什么顺序收、网申大概怎么走。它的用处是带路，不是替你决定以后读什么。

### 2. 同时申请的项目特别多

项目一多，截止日期、推荐信、文书版本、网申表格会一起挤过来。这个时候请人帮你盯流程、做清单、避免漏交，是合理的。帮忙的对象是事务，不是你的动机段。

## 不必请中介的情况

### 1. 先认清中介是什么

中介的本质是销售。不要默认对方文化水平更高，也不要默认对方能写出更好的文书。文书这件事，最好的版本一定是你自己写出来的：它必须能说出你自己在想什么。用 AI 帮你改病句、对一下结构，可以；把整篇交给中介去编，不行。中介通常不会去读你申请的那门课的大纲，而你最该做的，恰恰是把课程读一遍。

### 2. 课程匹配只能自己看

你把项目课程看完，才知道自己要不要上、跟自己的背景叠不叠得上。愿意做这一步的人，方向已经在自己手里。这个判断中介替不了你。如果你已经在看课、在想匹配度，你并不需要中介来告诉你该申什么。

### 3. 附加信息不要留给空白

不少网申有 additional information。中介可能略过这一栏，但对你的申请它经常是有用的：解释成绩、补充经历、把表格里塞不下的话写清楚。中介交完一稿之后，这一栏建议你自己写。

### 4. 愿意操心、也有时间的人，DIY 往往更直接

我自己是 DIY 的。对我来说，自己做效率更高：每一所的课、每一份表、每一段动机，都是我看过才交出去的。中介并不适合所有人。你有能力把流程 handle 住，也愿意花时间去操心，就不必把判断权交出去。

反过来：如果你对自己想做什么几乎没有想法，只想把申请丢给别人，中介很可能会按模板给你编一版文书。那种情况去找中介，风险在于交出去的并不是你。

## 一句话对照

- **可以找**：没申请过、时间挤、项目又多，需要人帮你认路、盯流程。
- **不必找**：你愿意读课程、想清楚匹配、文书要写自己的想法。附加信息自己补；中介做完之后，你仍要过一遍。

<div class="i18n-en">

The question comes up every season: whether to hire a study-abroad agency. Below I split it into when an agency can help, and when you do not need one. The point first: an agency can show you the path and keep the process in order. It cannot decide your direction or write a statement that is actually yours.

## When an agency can help

### 1. You have never applied, and time is short

If you have never submitted an application, and you are also sitting IELTS, GRE, and a final-year project, the pile is easy to lose order in. An agency can lay out the path: what this week is for, in what order to collect files, how the portal roughly works. Its use is to walk you through the process, not to decide what you should study.

### 2. You are applying to a large number of programmes

More programmes means more deadlines, references, statement versions, and forms at once. Paying someone to watch the calendar and stop a missed submit is reasonable. What they help with is the admin, not the motive paragraph.

## When you do not need one

### 1. An agency is a sales operation

Do not assume the counsellor is more literate than you, or that they will write a better statement. The best statement is the one you write. It has to say what you actually think. Using AI to clean sentences or check structure is fine. Handing the whole piece to an agency to invent is not. They usually will not read the course list of the programme you are applying to. Reading that list is the work you should not skip.

### 2. Fit with the curriculum is yours to judge

You only know whether you want the course, and whether it matches your background, after you have read it. If you are already doing that, the direction is already in your hands. An agency cannot make that call for you. If you are already reading modules and thinking about fit, you do not need an agency to tell you where to apply.

### 3. Do not leave additional information blank

Many portals have an additional-information box. An agency may skip it. For you it is often useful: a grade, an extra experience, a sentence that did not fit elsewhere. After they deliver a draft, write this box yourself.

### 4. If you can handle it and will put the time in, DIY is often cleaner

I applied DIY. For me it was more efficient: every course list, every form, every motive paragraph went out after I had read it. An agency is not for everyone. If you can run the process and you are willing to worry about the details, you do not need to hand the judgement over.

The other way around: if you have almost no idea what you want to do, and you only want to pass the application to someone else, an agency will often invent a statement from a template. Hiring one in that case means what you submit may not be you.

## In one line

- **Hire one** if you have never applied, time is tight, and the list of programmes is long: you need a path and a process.
- **Skip one** if you will read the courses, think about fit, and write a statement that is yours. Fill additional information yourself; still read through whatever the agency delivers.
